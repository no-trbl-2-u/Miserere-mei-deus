# 2026-10-23 — Every enemy in the game begins to move

> Enemies used to be one flat picture each, win or lose. Now they rest,
> flinch and lunge in time with the fight — starting with the Act 1 boss,
> who finally gets a real face instead of a blank placeholder, and a
> mid-game elite who traded four arms for a single wobbling body. Thirty
> cards in the Blood Price set got real names and real paintings, five new
> hazard cards that cost you something joined the puzzle workshop along
> with twenty puzzles built on them, and a die dropped low on a card on
> Android now actually lands. Most of the rest of tonight's work happened
> under the hood, tightening the project's own checks on itself.

## [mechanics] Every enemy now has motion

**What:** Enemies used to render as a single static picture no matter what
happened in the fight. They now play a resting loop between turns, flinch
when struck, and lunge on their own attack — timed off the exact same
moment combat already uses to decide whether a blow landed, so the motion
can never show something the fight didn't actually do. The work landed in
stages that changed no pixel until the very last step, so nothing was ever
shipped half-working.

**Why:** a still portrait read the same whether an attack hit or missed;
giving the enemy itself something to show closes that gap. T asked for the
work in this exact order — build the engine first, wire it to a real turn,
add the three states once it was visible, then generate art for enemies
already in the game — so each step could be checked before the next one
built on it.

**Commits:** `1008d3c7`, `de279d08`, `fa34a928`, `14fcefaa`, `6c1e50bb`

## [content] The Act 1 boss gets a face

**What:** The boss at the end of Act 1 shipped as a flat white placeholder
with two eye-shaped holes cut into it — a stand-in that had quietly become
the act's actual climax. It's now a fully painted undead beholder, The
Death Beholder, and it animates: it coils back and dims, then swells
forward on the very next frame for its attack. The fight underneath is
untouched — same level, same difficulty, same map placement, same loot —
because a separate measurement phase depends on this fight staying exactly
as it was.

**Why:** the placeholder had been the real boss for longer than intended;
this replaces the look without touching anything the game's own balance
numbers are measured against.

**Evidence:** foe enemy-death-beholder — new
**Commits:** `5ac5fe2e`, `3227fb9c`

## [content] A reflection becomes a jelly

**What:** The mid-region elite, formerly "the Brine Hag," is now "The
Green Jelly." T chose the new look after seeing it rigged for animation —
a single translucent body that squashes at its base needed none of the
four separate limb masks the Hag's arms did, and showed none of the seam
they left. It now breathes at rest, flattens when struck, and squashes
down before springing into its attack. The fight's level, loot, experience
bonus and "make friends with it instead of fighting" option all carry
over unchanged, including anyone who'd already befriended the Hag — their
save quietly points at the Jelly now instead.

**Why:** an art change made for animation, not for the fight itself; the
save-compatibility work was necessary because, unlike the Act 1 boss, this
enemy can be befriended, and that choice has to survive the rename.

**Evidence:** foe enemy-green-jelly — new
**Commits:** `5117001a8`, `bf677e86b`

## [mechanics] Combat loses a pop-up, and a flinch learns to wait

**What:** Two small fixes T caught watching the first preview build
carrying the new animation work. The floating card that used to hover
centered over the enemy for about two seconds after every round is gone —
the existing threat icons, damage numbers and combat log already say what
happened, so the card was saying it twice. And the enemy's "struck" flinch
now waits for the player's own played card to finish moving across the
board before it plays, instead of firing immediately and getting half-hidden
behind it.

**Why:** both were things T noticed getting in the way on a real build,
not planned polish — the kind of thing that only shows up once the motion
itself exists to watch.

**Commits:** `8b6b4c8b2`, `837c8c7c`

## [content] Thirty Blood Price cards get names, and paintings

**What:** The last 25 placeholder-named cards in the Blood Price set
("Blood Price P-01a" and so on) now carry real names read off what each

**Evidence:** card bp-e-02d, bp-e-03d, bp-e-04c, bp-e-06b, bp-e-07b,

## [mechanics] Five hazard cards that cost you something, and twenty puzzles built on them

**What:** Five new cards for the hazard mini-game trade meter progress, or
a die, for a benefit — a toll, a debt, an overdrive. T audited the first
version and ruled that playing one of these unpowered must always have its
own reason, not just be a worse version of playing it powered; their
numbers were reworked so that holds everywhere. Twenty new puzzles (ten
medium, ten hard) built on these cards replace the twenty easy/medium
puzzles that didn't use them, and are now loaded onto the developer-only
puzzle review board.

**Why:** this is what the "T plays the thirty" demo (due 2026-11-02) is
waiting on — with tonight's puzzles shipped, that demo's set is finally
real and ready for T to play.

**Shot:** devpuzzle-list — the developer puzzle board, now listing the
real ten-hard-plus-twenty-cost-card set (this is a developer-only screen,
not reachable from a normal playthrough)

## [ui] A die dropped low on a card now lands, on Android

**What:** on Android, dropping a die onto the lower half of a staged card
in either the hazard or combat board could silently miss. Android's
edge-to-edge display shifts how the game was measuring a card's position
by exactly one status bar's height, so the game and the drag disagreed
about where the card actually was. Both boards now measure a drop the same
way the drag itself is measured.

**Why:** T found this on a real preview-build run; it affected hitting
either board, not one screen.

**Commits:** `ad05ae5a`

## [infra] The merge gate learns five new ways to catch a broken pull request

**What:** the automatic check every change passes before it can merge now
also: checks that the project's own planning files are shaped the way the
tools expect; refuses to pass a change that touches a folder no automatic
test is watching; requires that any pull request claiming a finished task
point at a real, checkable test as proof, not just a ticked box; re-runs
any newly added test against the code as it stood before the change, so a
test that would have passed either way (and so proves nothing) gets
flagged instead of counted as new coverage; and starts keeping score of how
often the project's own AI playtesting pass actually catches the bugs it's
shown.

**Why:** each one closes a real gap the project had already hit — a
queue file that got silently corrupted by a bad merge, a folder nothing
was watching, a "done" checkbox with no evidence behind it, and tests that
look like new coverage but aren't.

**Commits:** `ef7aa0d8`, `deff52b3`, `13eee0a4`, `b8aa9f99`, `69b6753a`

## [infra] The feature map starts mapping itself

**What:** a script now reads every screen and every engine area in the
game and builds a map of how they connect — what leads to what, what's
reachable, what's actually been played through by a real test, and what's
missing a design brief — then renders it as both a browsable table and a
visual diagram. Its first real run found five screens that are technically
reachable but, right now, only through a developer shortcut, not a normal
playthrough.

**Why:** keeps the project's map of its own game honest without anyone
having to redraw it by hand every time a screen is added, removed or
rewired.

**Commits:** `47d0e494`, `ddd6961a`, `e4714e41`, `13c4612c`, `879d4d81`

## [infra] Visual changes now carry their own before-and-after picture

**What:** whenever a change moves pixels on a screen, the project's own
automated checks now take the before and after screenshots themselves and
attach them to the change for review — so a difference can be seen, not
just trusted. A second piece makes the version of this check that runs on
a developer's own machine shoot only the screens a given change could
plausibly affect, instead of every screen every time.

**Why:** this project's visual check had gone stale before without anyone
noticing — one screen's reference picture sat untouched for weeks — because
nothing forced a look at the actual pixels before approving a change.

**Commits:** `f7746930`, `69cebccad`

## [infra] Art tools get their own home, sealed off from the shipped app

**What:** the Python tools that build enemy animation sheets, and the
original master art files they work from, now live in a new top-level
folder of their own, separate from the phone app's codebase. A new check
makes sure nothing in that folder is ever pulled into the app or shipped in
a build.

**Why:** the tooling used to live inside the app's own folder even though
it isn't code the app's checks can verify, and a folder of master art that
must never ship needs an enforced boundary, not a trusted one — the
project has already been burned once by a boundary nobody checked.

**Commits:** `4d37d3b4`, `58e81c3c`

## [infra] Hazard-puzzle solve times move off the phone and onto a server

**What:** a small backend service now records every puzzle attempt a
developer preview build makes — its layout, how it ended, how long it
took — to a real database instead of just the one phone it happened on. A
new report reads solve rate and solve time back out for every puzzle,
grouped by how hard it's meant to be.

**Why:** solve data used to live only on whichever phone ran it, with no
way to compare puzzles against each other; this is what a later phase will
use to set each puzzle's timer from real play instead of a guess.

**Commits:** `170b2906`, `8e64732f`, `8113899e`, `4dfe53c2`

## [infra] Two small loop-housekeeping fixes

**What:** the project's own build automation stopped capping how many
pull requests can merge in one day — a finished, reviewed change sometimes
had to wait simply because twelve others had already landed that day, even
though nothing about it was actually risky. Separately, when two planning
sessions add new rows to the same plan file around the same time, a new
merge rule now keeps both rows instead of silently dropping one, which used
to need a person to notice and fix by hand.

**Why:** the daily cap had never actually been written into the project's
own rulebook, so it was enforcing a rule nobody had decided; the plan-file
conflict had already bitten the project three times in two days before the
fix landed.

**Commits:** `140064a2`, `34e8f9dd`
