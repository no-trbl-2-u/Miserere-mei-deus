> Character selection makes its first appearance: a run will soon open on a
> screen where you pick who you are, though no one is on the roster yet to
> pick. Equipment gets a real third slot built just for relics instead of
> one vague catch-all, and a relic now tells you outright which lane of
> cards it unlocks while you wear it. A region's elite enemy can come back
> after you rest there — same fight, same reward, until its one relic has
> dropped, then it's gone for good. Every card's old "(max +n)" print is
> gone from the two live families; a cap only appears from now on if a
> designer calls for one by name. And the card-vote ballots stopped

## [mechanics] Character selection makes its first appearance

**What:** the engine now has a real idea of "a character" — a portrait, a
starting relic, two starting card lanes, three starting lane cards and a
base stat split — and a new run built from one wears its relic and deals
its cards automatically (phase CH1a). The card-reward pool during a run is
now the character's own two starting lanes plus whatever relics' lanes you
currently have worn, rather than just the worn relics alone (CH1b). A
fighting-game-style select screen exists now too: a carousel of square
portraits, locked or unlocked (CH1c). And the project's own measured-truth
report gained a "reward lanes by character" section, so a look at the
numbers can show exactly what cards a given character would see (CH1d).
Nobody is on the roster yet — the character list is still empty, and the
two phases that put a real person there (T authoring the first one, then
wiring it into new games) are waiting on a third card family existing to
choose from.

**Why:** T's ruling at the character-selection pitch (D108): "each run
should open with an identity, and late-run lane control should differ from
the start." This is the first piece of a from-the-ground-up answer to "who
am I playing," not a cosmetic skin on the run that exists today.

**No capture:** the select screen (CH1c) shipped behind its own route, but
with the roster empty there is nothing in it to show yet, and no baseline
screenshot for it has been captured and approved.
**Commits:** `4a9b16eb8`, `d24cd6cd6`, `bf9f07ec7`, `931bfd3dc`

## [mechanics] Relics get a real home, and wearing one promises its cards

**What:** equipment now has three honest slots — one Weapon, one Armor,
and three for Relics — replacing the old single "Accessory" slot that
relics, trinkets and rings all had to share (EQ2a); saves made before this
change carry over automatically. Every screen that used to say "Trinket"
or "Accessory" now says "Weapon," "Armor" or "Relic" (EQ2b). The Bleeding
Signet's own detail view now names its lane outright ("Lane: Blood Price,
while worn"), and every other lane relic says the same about its own lane
(LR1a). Elite and quest fights can now actually hand you one of those
relics as a drop (an 8% chance on an elite win), instead of the one
starting ring being the only way to ever see one (LR1c). The fight log now
records which relics and signatures you had worn into a fight, feeding a
new signature-usage report, and a signature refused for a reason the game
can explain now logs that reason too (LR1g). Riding along: a long-standing
bug meant every fresh run started with a ring already worn for you; now a
fresh run starts with none, and any relic the game hands you afterward
wears itself automatically if a slot is free (SG1).

**Why:** a card reward has drawn from your worn relics' lanes since
10-25's reward-rule change, but until this window a relic was just one
undifferentiated slot that never told you what wearing it would do, and
the only relic in the game was a hardcoded freebie. This window gives
relics a real place to live, a way to actually earn more of them, and the
plain-language promise ("Lane: X, while worn") that tells you why putting
one on matters.

**No capture:** the slot rename and relic-drop plumbing are engine and
screen-wording changes with no committed baseline screenshot this window,
and neither touches card, foe or affliction data the catalog tool diffs.
**Commits:** `f0c8e8b44`, `cce4cf345`, `2553b4580`, `01f02f53d`,
`e6e20d828`, `c74ca5263`, `ca102a2b7`, `e7a1033c8`

## [mechanics] A region's elite can fight you again after you rest

**What:** resting at a region's rest spot now brings its elite enemy back
for another round — same foe, same level, same full reward (XP and your
pick of cards, or an upgrade). Each time you beat it, it rolls its own
chance (8%) to drop its one relic; once that relic has dropped, that
elite is retired for good and stops coming back. Old saves keep working
unchanged, no conversion needed.

**Why:** T's own task for this phase: "elite enemies respawn after a rest
node is triggered." It turns a region's elite from a one-and-done fight
into a real, if capped, source of repeatable XP and card picks — the
brief that scoped it was explicit that this is meant as a farm, with the
relic side kept finite on purpose.

**No capture:** the rest screen's new "<elite> is back" line has no
committed baseline screenshot from this window.
**Commits:** `db22fcc4e`

## [balance] No more printed caps on cards, and ballots stop pitching colour

**What:** every "(max +n)" that used to print on a Blood Price or
Elementalist card's upgrade line is gone — a card only carries a cap now
if a designer calls for one by name (the two existing SACRIFICE caps
stayed, by T's own call). One line whose only way of growing on upgrade
*was* its cap (Repaid in Kind) now grows by a flat amount instead, and its
paid effect became twice the damage you've taken. Separately, the
card-voting ballot tool dropped its colour pitch entirely: it no longer
proposes or tints a job colour for a candidate card, and `/lane-creator`
now proposes splitting a new family's colours evenly only after the cards
themselves are picked, for the designer to adjust by hand.

**Why:** two blanket rulings from T (2026-10-26). On caps (D109): "remove
the '(max +n)' from every card. If there should be a max, I'll call it
out." On colour (D110): "a card's colour is ONLY what die pays for them
and is not categorized otherwise... I pick the cards, then afterwards, it
can split the colors up evenly." Both are rules set once, in T's own
words, rather than re-litigated card by card or vote by vote.

**Evidence:** card bp-m-02b — reprinted; card bp-m-10a — reprinted; card
bp-p-06d — reprinted; card bp-p-07b — reprinted; card el-p-06b —
reprinted (5 of the affected cards; the rest changed the same way)
**Commits:** `22f70be05`, `b2bd7bb55`

## [ui] The board and the reward screens read more plainly

**What:** the combat board now spells out more of what it's doing: your
own INFERNO reads as yours with its per-turn loss shown, an incoming hit
that BLIZZARD will cut says so on its intent line, an all-MISS turn's
dead tray still shows END following through, and every number that floats
off a hit now has its own readable timing instead of arriving in a blur.
Elsewhere: the reward cache's "TAKE A CARD" offer now shows the actual
card face before you commit to it, instead of asking you to pick blind;
grabbing spoils takes two taps instead of several, and a map node's gold
total shows right on its panel; and a tap-to-open glossary now explains
Conviction, dice, piles and the phase ledger right on the board, reachable
by a sighted player without leaving the screen.

**Why:** each of these closes a small but real "wait, what just happened"
gap — a hidden INFERNO tick, a blind card pick, a buried gold total, game
terms with nowhere to look them up. None of them change what a fight or a
reward does; they change whether the screen is honest about it.

**No capture:** none of these screens' baselines changed in this window's
committed captures, so no before/after is available tonight.
**Commits:** `17459ddf8`, `4b512e402`, `8e6aa246e`, `76af5f193`

## [infra] The project's own testing tools get sharper

**What:** the fresh-eyes playtester agent now drives its own dedicated
browser through a shared session tool instead of borrowed Playwright
tools, so the same four-way swarm it already ran stays intact on a
cleaner foundation (PH1a, PH1b). The written playtest guides for hazards,
combat and the minigames are now checked by a test against the actual ids
and modules they describe, so a guide drifting from the real game gets
caught instead of silently going stale (PH1c). A new script walks a
complete Act 1 crossing — all four regions, door to exit — and proves
every kind of event the region promises actually gets hit along the way,
catching both code defects and filing content ones it can't fix itself
(AW1a). And a companion test checks that every map node's own blurb
actually matches the event that node triggers (AW1b).

**Why:** each of these is the project checking that its own checks are
trustworthy — a playtest guide that quietly drifted from the game, or a
map node whose blurb promised the wrong fight, are exactly the kind of gap
that would otherwise only surface when a player hit it first.
**Commits:** `8e9717b32`, `d795b3b4a`, `698884e0f`, `087b8b0bf`,
`07b521300`

## [infra] The iOS beta pipeline can submit itself

**What:** the app now has real iOS build configuration and a written
runbook for enrolling, building and shipping a TestFlight beta (IO1a); the
preview-build workflow can now take a "submit" input that, only for an iOS
beta build, writes the App Store Connect API key to a one-time file and
runs the build with auto-submit turned on, so a beta build can reach
testers' phones without a manual App Store Connect upload (IO1c).

**Why:** gets the project from "a build exists" to "a build reaches a
tester's phone" without a manual step in between, ahead of the iPhone
enrollment decision still waiting on T (`plot` PR #803).
**Commits:** `6428ad76b`, `74b396611`

## [infra] Measured truth adds the Elementalist's own numbers

**What:** the nightly measured-truth report now samples two Elementalist
variants directly — a 20-of-30 draw from its own cards, and a 20-of-60
draw mixed with other families — rather than only ever reporting its
fixed 20-card test deck, and every cell now also reports how much VITAE a
deck lost to its own INFERNO ticks (self-burn), separate from damage taken
from the enemy.

**Why:** the Elementalist is the first lane whose own cards can hurt the
player who's playing it; without a self-burn column, a death from your own
fire and a death from the enemy's hit would read identically in the
numbers.
**Commits:** `768dc7bf2`
