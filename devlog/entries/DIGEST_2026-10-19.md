## [ui] The hazard puzzle preview now plays on the real hazard screen

**What:** The developer-only hazard puzzle demo used to be its own
simplified stand-in: its own button layout, its own outcome panel, its own
everything. It now renders through the exact same board, route-select
screen and outcome panel a real hazard encounter uses on a normal
playthrough — the same drag-and-drop card handling, the same dice tray,
the same "CONFIRM" resolve flow. It is still only reachable from the
developer menu, not from a normal playthrough; the puzzle's rules
(fixed layout, no randomness) haven't changed.

**Why:** the puzzle mechanic was proven out on a throwaway UI first so its
rules could be nailed down without the real screen's complexity in the
way. Moving it onto the live board now is the planned next step before it
can be unlocked for players: whatever ships to a real hazard encounter has
already been exercised on this exact screen.

## [mechanics] The puzzle drops its scrap bin and starts counting wins honestly

**What:** Three small engine changes to the hazard puzzle: cards can no
longer be discarded into a scrap bin mid-puzzle (the bin itself is gone
from the puzzle board); a "win" is now counted only from the cards that
actually changed the result — a real value, a die, or an effect — instead
of crediting every card a player happened to play; and cards can now carry
negative costs, hand out a temporary die, or turn one or all of a player's
dice to a fixed face.

**Why:** the puzzle is meant to be a fixed, fully solved layout with no
randomness and no do-over, and a scrap bin plus an inflated win count both
undercut that guarantee — a card that did nothing could still count toward
clearing the puzzle. The new signed costs and the two new card effects
(gaining a die, rerolling dice to a set face) extend the vocabulary future
puzzle cards can use, ahead of the first real cards being authored against
it.

#628, #631, #633)

## [infra] The loop's own pull requests now get an automatic review verdict

**What:** Every pull request the automated build loop opens now gets
reviewed by a dedicated check once its other tests pass. A low-risk change
gets one reviewer pass; a medium- or high-risk one gets three separate
review angles — rules compliance, test coverage, and how far the change's
blast radius reaches — merged into a single verdict comment on the PR. A
new seeded test plants known bugs on purpose and checks whether the
reviewer's verdict actually catches them, feeding a running "miss rate"
table the team can watch over time.

**Why:** the automated test suite alone doesn't catch everything a human
reviewer would (sequencing mistakes, rules that look right but aren't);
this gives every loop-authored change a second, independent check before a
person has to look at it, and measures — rather than assumes — how well
that check actually works.

**Commits:** `864407e42`, `3bf5830b1`, `223e977dd`, `bb1eb1dc0` (phases
RV1b–RV1c, PRs #602, #610)

**No capture:** a CI review pipeline and a verdict comment on pull
requests; nothing a player sees in the game.

## [ui] Six small fixes from the project's own nightly testing pass

**What:** The project runs an unattended testing pass each night that
plays through the game's screens looking for rough edges. Over the last
two nights it caught, and the loop fixed: the life-total readout
announcing itself twice to a screen reader instead of once; a title-screen
menu that could spill outside its own frame on a wide desktop window; a
duplicated status label in combat reading out twice; a caption under
combat's SWAY/CONVICTION icon rendering too small to read and ignoring the
system text-size setting; and, across the hub, the hazard screen, a reward
panel, a cutscene and the map, on-screen instructions that told every
player to "tap" now say "choose" or "continue" instead, regardless of
whether they're using a touchscreen, a mouse, or a keyboard.

**Why:** these are the project's own fresh-eyes testing pass (critique
passes 92 and 93) doing its job — catching small legibility,
duplicate-announcement and wording inconsistencies before a real player
hits them.

## [infra] A standing reminder keeps deadlines from slipping unnoticed

**What:** The project's deadline plan now tracks each upcoming item in one
table (what's due, what it needs, which build-plan phases cover it). A new
script regenerates a short list daily of only the items that still need
the maintainer's attention within the next two weeks — something with no
plan yet, or a task sitting on their desk — and prints that list first
thing when they start a new chat session.

**Why:** a deadline with no owner or no plan behind it can slip by
unnoticed in a project this size; this surfaces exactly what needs a
decision, and nothing else, before any other work starts.
