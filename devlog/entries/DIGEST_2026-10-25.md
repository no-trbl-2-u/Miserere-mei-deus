# 2026-10-25 — Every card in your deck can grow stronger

> Cards can now be upgraded — once, twice or three times depending on how
> rare they are — at the blacksmith's anvil or at a rest site, and an elite
> enemy's reward finally offers a real choice instead of a fixed pool. A
> second trial card family, the Elementalist, arrived with weather that
> turns against both sides mid-fight: fire, frost and storm. Every Spell in
> the game now costs you the card itself if you cast it for free, which
> made every Spell's paid cast a little stronger to balance. And the
> equipment screen stopped lying about what you're wearing — you can
> finally take a relic back off. Underneath all of that, the project built
> itself a backend that watches every fight and every deck change as it
> happens, then reused it to let the designer vote on new cards from his
> phone.

## [mechanics] Every card in your deck can grow stronger

**What:** Any card can now be upgraded — common cards once, uncommon twice,
rare three times — and every upgrade only raises what the card already
does, never what it costs, marked `+`, `++` or `+++` on its face. One
shared screen shows the before-and-after preview with a locked "???" slot
for the next step still to come. There are two ways to get there in a run:
pay 5 shillings at the blacksmith's anvil, once per visit, alongside the
existing HONE, TEMPER and SWAP options, or take a free upgrade offered at
a rest site instead of resting or cutting a card. An elite enemy's reward
now offers a real choice — the best 3 cards actually available, or a
random upgrade of your best-rarity owned card — instead of a fixed pool.
And the set's first rare card, Blood for Blood, jumped to a bigger, harder
rank and can now take all three upgrade steps.

**Why:** this is the build this project owes by 2026-11-17 under its own
deadline plan, and it shipped more than three weeks early. It gives a
reason to keep a card you'd otherwise replace, and turns an elite fight's
reward from a fixed slot machine into an actual decision.

**Evidence:** card bp-e-08n (Blood for Blood, now rank 5, a rare) —
reprinted

## [content] A second card family arrives, and it brings weather with it

**What:** the Elementalist is a new trial lane built around five weather
conditions that can turn against either side mid-fight: INFERNO burns both
you and the foe each turn, BLIZZARD sets up FROZEN (the next hit lands
doubled), STORM grows stronger every turn it's left standing, and DRAW
pulls weather toward neutral. Each one has its own gloss, popup, board
number and log line, so a fight under weather reads differently from one
without it. Thirty cards, still carrying placeholder names and coloured by
job, now sit in a new `elementalist` lane and its reward pool, with a
20-card test deck built from whichever of the thirty played best in the
design simulations.

**Why:** the Elementalist is the second card family this project has
designed end to end through the same process that produced Blood Price —
a ballot T votes on from his phone, with the verdicts read back into a
database. T ratified that process as official partway through this very
family's design ("I LOVE this process so far"), so the Elementalist is
also the first test of the newly-written rulebook for building the next
one.

**No capture:** the thirty Elementalist cards are new to the catalog, but
this run's card-evidence tool caps itself at 8 before/after plates per
night and spent all 8 on Blood Price's own changes (below); no plate
exists yet for an Elementalist card specifically.

## [mechanics] Every Spell now costs you something when you can't pay for it

**What:** every Spell card in the game — in every lane — now ends its
free-to-play line with EXILE, meaning the card leaves your deck for the
rest of the run if you cast it that way, while its paid line never does.
To keep that fair, every Spell's paid payoff went up by at least one
upgrade step. The rule now covers all 17 of the game's trial-lane Spells;
the grey starter deck's own Spell, A Plain Word, is held back until T
rules on a side effect the law has on an early Act 1 door's win rate.

**Why:** a single rule T set for every Spell across the game (D103), built
exactly as stated rather than judged card by card — the kind of rule a
designer sets once instead of relitigating for every new lane.

**Evidence:** card bp-e-01n, bp-e-04c, bp-e-07b, bp-e-10d, bp-m-07a,
bp-m-10a, bp-p-04c — reprinted (7 of the 17 affected Spells shown; the
rest changed the same way)
**Commits:** `06a1e8ed` (measured regenerate), `4b4ad3bf`

## [mechanics] The equipment screen finally tells the truth

**What:** every mobile screen that says an item is worn now reads from the
exact same place the fight itself reads from, instead of a separate list
that could quietly disagree with it. A worn relic can now be taken off
(UNEQUIP) from either the satchel or its own detail screen, and an
unworn one can be put on (EQUIP) the same way — starting a fight with two
relics worn now carries both of their effects. A signature refused for a
reason other than low Conviction, like a monster the game won't let you
tame, is now marked refused on the column and explains why as soon as
you tap it. Two smaller fixes rode along: TETHER's sacrifice-triggered hit
wasn't consuming FROZEN the way every other hit does, and an action button
was overhanging its row on the equipment detail card.

**Why:** the inventory-order list and the fight's own equipment state
could already drift apart — one screen could say an item was worn while
the fight disagreed — and until now a relic, once equipped, had no way
back off. Both are the kind of gap that erodes trust in what a screen is
telling you.

**Commits:** `7c7b8830`, `9d2e07ca`, `66b1257e`, `0a08e36b`

## [infra] A backend behind the game: first for fights, then for voting on cards

**What:** a small Cloudflare Worker and database now record, from a
developer preview build, every fight's full event stream (what was
played, when, and the VITAE cost) and every deck change (a reward offered,
picked or skipped; a rest-site cut) as it happens, with two reports
(`card-usage`, `card-gaps`) reading it back. The same backend was then
reused for an entirely different job: `axiomancer-ballots`, a phone site
where T votes on card candidates, autosaving every tick and note and
submitting a verdict that can be saved unfinished and resumed later. Ballot
candidates now also carry a colour by job, so a vote on thirty cards reads
consistently at a glance.

**Why:** solve and play data used to live only on whichever phone produced
it; this is the infrastructure a later phase needs to compare card
performance for real. Reusing the same pipe for ballots, rather than
building a second one, is what let the Elementalist's vote happen on the
same day T asked for it.

**Commits:** `8824b129`, `12a044ee`, `143008a2`, `807531a6`, `00dff92c`,
`49c70e0c`

## [infra] The feature map zooms in, and mirrors itself into the vault

**What:** the project's self-generated map of every screen and engine area
now writes one note per feature (with player-flow links and coupling

**Why:** keeps the project's map of its own game readable as it grows,
and puts it where T already reads his own notes, instead of a second
place he'd have to remember to check.

## [infra] The project's checks on itself get sharper

**What:** a `judge` job now re-runs each finished phase's own named proof
against the actual PR before letting it merge, instead of trusting the
checkbox; a report-only mutation-testing pass scores how well a PR's
tests would catch a deliberately broken version of its own changed lines;
25 combat-board tests and 12 state/presenter tests now start from a real
booted fight instead of a hand-built mock, which is now deleted; and
hand-written package reports that used to sit unfiled now get swept
automatically into the fresh-eyes queue. Separately, the project's own
fresh-eyes tool had been screenshotting three screens mid-fade-in since a
boot-flag bug flagged at pass 99 — fixed this morning, closing the HIGH
finding the same tick it was raised.

**Why:** a test that would pass whether or not the fix worked proves
nothing, and a mock standing in for a real fight can quietly drift from
what the engine actually does — both are exactly the kind of gap that
let past regressions through unnoticed. The fresh-eyes fix matters for
every future critique pass: a tool blind on three of eleven screens was
reporting that nothing changed when it genuinely couldn't see.

**Commits:** `e12b7dfd`, `06a1e8ed`, `2ded2fd1`, `39b474ef`, `7b5e1bc8`,
`70877f90`
