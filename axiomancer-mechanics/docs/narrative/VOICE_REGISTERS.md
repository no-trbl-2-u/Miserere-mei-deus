# Voice Registers

Registers are functional constraints, not filters applied to every line. A
screen may move between them, but labels and rules remain visually separate
from prose.

## 1. World narration — the field register

**Purpose:** establish location, usable conditions, and evidence of prior lives.

- Camera stays near hand-height: footing, reach, sound source, worked surfaces.
- Name two concrete facts before one interpretation.
- Landscape acts by physics and accumulated alteration, not human emotion.
- Typical shape: `observable condition. Human trace. Present consequence.`

**Good lock:** “Roots have lifted the road stones into shallow ribs. Resin plugs
three old survey cuts. The cart must take the lower path.”

**Avoid:** omniscient moral labels, mood-mirroring weather, scenic inventories,
or unexplained grandeur.

## 2. Combat — the pressure register

**Purpose:** let the player read threat, status interaction, and timing under
pressure.

- Use active subject–verb–object clauses.
- Put threat name, target, amount, and timing first.
- Flavor may follow after an em dash or in a separate one-line beat.
- Preserve **VITAE** and **STANCE** exactly.
- Status names and stack/duration language outrank metaphor.

**Pattern:** `THREAT: [action] — [target/effect/timing].`

**Example:** `THREAT: Root-Shear — lose 4 VITAE after this phase. BLEED advances
by 1.`

## 3. Hazard/Event encounter — the choice register

**Purpose:** turn place into a legible decision.

Order: (1) condition, (2) evidence, (3) choices, (4) explicit mechanical
previews, (5) result. Choice labels begin with verbs and do not smuggle a moral
judgment into “good” or “evil” wording. Unknown outcomes are marked `Unknown`,
not disguised.

Use one brief return line after resolution to show residue in the world.

## 4. Item descriptions — the handled register

**Purpose:** make an object memorable without hiding utility.

- Line 1: material, construction, wear.
- Line 2: owner/use/provenance, stated only as evidence permits.
- `Effect:` line: exact rule in canonical terms.
- Prefer specific repair over mystical adjective.

**Example:** “A wedge of pine blackened at one end, wrapped twice in sailcloth.
Someone marked each successful crossing with a notch.

`Effect: Remove 1 ROOT. Single use.`”

## 5. Character dialogue — speaker-owned registers

Every recurring speaker receives a voice card with:

| Field | Required decision |
|---|---|
| Goal in scene | what the speaker wants now |
| Knowledge boundary | what they know, assume, and refuse to claim |
| Answer order | direct answer first, story first, question first, bargaining first |
| Sentence pressure | clipped, accumulating, corrective, digressive, exacting |
| Concrete domain | fishing, accounts, masonry, medicine, court procedure, etc. |
| Evasion habit | silence, joke, counteroffer, definition, changed subject |
| Heat signal | what changes when afraid or angry |
| Forbidden habit | a trait reserved for another voice |

**Existing lock:** Old Marrow remains laconic, work-minded, and sparing with
exclamation. Do not distribute that voice to the Father, the Boy, merchants,
hermits, or court officials.

The Boy may name immediate wants and revise himself aloud. An official may hide
agency in procedure. A craftsperson may answer through sequence and tolerances.
Those are examples, not universal assignments.

## 6. UI/mechanical copy — the instrument register

**Purpose:** make the next valid action unmistakable.

- Use labels, not lore: `END PHASE`, `Lose 3 VITAE`, `Applies POISON (2)`.
- Use one term for one mechanic; follow the canonical project lexicon.
- State costs before confirmation and results after resolution.
- Use numerals for quantities and rounds.
- Do not introduce diegetic synonyms for buttons, statuses, resources, or
  accessibility text.
- Error copy says what failed and how to recover.

**Example:** `Cannot play: requires 1 MIND die.`

## 7. Lore/codex — the record register

**Purpose:** expand history while preserving uncertainty and source conflict.

Use a four-part entry when possible:

1. **Object/place:** what exists now.
2. **Record:** who says what, and where the account came from.
3. **Conflict:** incompatible evidence or interpretation.
4. **Use:** why the player may care now.

Codex voice is plain and source-aware. It may report a ritual title, inscription,
or testimony, but does not certify institutions' self-descriptions. “Unknown”
is better than counterfeit mystery.

## Register transition rule

Never blend a poetic sentence into the clickable choice or rules line. Separate
layers typographically:

> Resin runs from the cut as the trunk leans across the ford.
>
> **Brace the trunk** — Spend 1 BODY die. Prevent 3 VITAE loss.

This separation is mandatory for localization, accessibility, and play clarity.
