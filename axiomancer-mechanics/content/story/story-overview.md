## Rulings

1. **The source.** The story is written from the four Act 1 maps (the
   Breakwater, the Charcoal Wood, the Beacon Crags, the Lantern Deep) and
   nothing from the story that stood here before.
2. **The premise.** The fire God hung over the world to see it by was taken
   down, cut up and sold. A penitent walks the chain of dead lights from the
   sea to the deep to be seen and forgiven, and the voice telling it is the
   one who carried the fire down.
3. **The world.** There was one Lamp. It burned without fuel and nothing
   under it cast a shadow or went unseen. It was lowered on a chain, divided,
   and bought by those who could pay. Every light in the land now is a piece
   of it or a copy of it, and every one is owed for.
4. **The penitent (the player).** Wants to stand under the Lamp, be seen, and
   be forgiven for a thing done before the story starts. Needs to learn that
   no eye is left to see it: mercy now has no witness and is given in the
   dark, by hand, or not at all. The spare-or-finish choice is this, each
   time. The penitent has no name and no stated sex.
5. **The Keeper is the Narrator.** The narrator figure and voice (spec 177)
   is a character, the Keeper: the last lamplighter, who kept the tally of
   the lights and was paid to lower the first one. He tells the story to the
   penitent as testimony, never lies about what he did, and leaves it out.
6. **The Keeper's guilt is revealed at the deep stair,** at the end of Act 1.
   Earlier chapters plant it.
7. **The carried pieces.** The penitent takes the piece of the Lamp each lord
   holds and carries it on. The Keeper guides the penitent because he needs
   the pieces brought down to him and cannot carry them himself. The pieces
   are story only: no item or mechanic.
8. **The lords.** Each chapter ends on someone who holds a piece of the Lamp
   and has made a small kingdom in its circle. Each one paid for it.
9. **The Saint Beneath is the lord of the sea keep.** He bought the harbour's
   lamp. The town prays to him because the wrecks feed it. He stays the
   Breakwater's boss where he already fights; he is pivotal to Chapter 1
   only. Every other enemy name is open to change, and none is changed yet.
10. **Chapter 1's mechanics stay as built:** the four-step quest chain, its
    node placement, the key, the keep gate and the bridge gate. Every name
    and line on them is new.
11. **People speak.** Scenes may contain people. The Keeper narrates and each
    person speaks inside his telling in their own voice, without quotation
    marks. This replaces the rule that the Narrator never writes speech; the
    narrate skill and spec 177 are amended when this story is plotted.
12. **Cruelty.** Scenes rotate through the stops 1, 2, 1, 3, 1, 4, 1, 5 and
    round again. A child's death is named and never shown.

## Act 1, map by map

Each map asks one question and answers it.

| Map | The question | The answer | Its lord | The Keeper there |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| The Breakwater | Who put out the light? | No one. It was sold. The lord in the sea keep bought it, burns it where only the keep can see, and lives on what the rocks bring in. | Bought the harbour light and thinks he is keeping it safe. | Courteous, exact, a guide. Says nothing of himself. |
| The Charcoal Wood | What burns in its place? | Whatever will. The burners sell fire by the night, and the wood they cut grows from a burying ground. | Buried his piece in the oldest kiln because he could not bear what it showed him. | Begins to speak of the Lamp as something he once stood under. |
| The Beacon Crags | Who is the warning for? | Not the valley. The beacon faces down, to tell what lies under the mountain that someone is on the stair carrying fire. | A faithful servant who keeps a signal for a master he has never seen. | Slips: describes the inside of the mountain before the penitent has seen it. |
| The Lantern Deep | What is the light for? | To keep something asleep. The largest piece hangs over a giant that sleeps only while it is lit, and it is failing. | The city's creditor, who holds every lamp's debt. The Act 1 boss. | Present in the flesh at the head of the deep stair. He asks for the pieces. Act 1 ends on the asking. |

- The Breakwater ends with the lighthouse still dark, the bridge open, and a
  fire lit on a mountain inland that was not burning before.
- In the Beacon Crags a monastery keeps its rule in the dark and will not buy
  a light. Its brothers offer the penitent the only other road; it is refused.
- At the deep stair the Keeper has counted every mercy and every finishing.
  The penitent is seen at last, by the one witness left, who forgives nothing.
- A sealed vault and the stair below are Act 2.

**No scene depends on a node the player can skip.** A scene plays on first
arrival whatever the quest state, so each scene on the lighthouse, the keep
gate and the river bridge describes the place and the person and is true on
an early visit; the ordered story sits in the quest lines, which the engine
gates. Four scenes lean on earlier ones and the graph forces each order: the
moored ship, the cave face and the fishermen's camp follow the wreck; the sea
keep needs the key; the far bank needs the keep beaten. The mandatory road is
27 nodes and carries 16 of the 22 scenes.
